England midfielder Jordan Henderson has had surgery on a broken arm in Kansas City and will sit out the remainder of the World Cup, the team confirmed on Tuesday.

The 36-year-old was hurt not during play but in the celebrations that followed England's 3-2 comeback win over Mexico on Sunday night. Climbing back over an advertising hoarding after joining supporters behind the goal, Henderson lost his footing, landed heavily on his arm, and left the stadium on a stretcher as a precaution.

He remained in the host city overnight before rejoining the squad at their Kansas City base, where the operation took place on Tuesday morning. "Surgery done! Now let's get ready for the big one Saturday," the Brentford midfielder wrote on social media afterwards.

Although his tournament is over as a player, Henderson has asked to stay with the group through the quarterfinal against Norway in Miami on Saturday, a request the coaching staff is expected to grant.

His absence thins England's options in central midfield, leaving Declan Rice, Elliot Anderson, Jude Bellingham and Kobbie Mainoo to cover the remaining matches.

Henderson's appearance in the final group game against Panama made him the first men's player to represent England at four World Cups, a milestone he described at the time as the proudest moment of his international career.
