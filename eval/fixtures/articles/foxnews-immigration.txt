Border agents are sounding the alarm over what one union official called an "unprecedented" surge at a remote crossing this month, warning that resources are stretched thin. The official said apprehensions had climbed sharply, though the agency has not released official figures for the period. Local business owners told this outlet they are frustrated by the federal response. Critics of the administration seized on the reports as evidence its policies are failing. A department spokesperson disputed the characterization, saying staffing had actually increased. The union did not provide documentation for its figures, and independent verification was not available by press time.
